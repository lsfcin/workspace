# drawing
> Pictures built from shapes — diagrams, graphs, arrows, scenes: refined and minimal, never lazy.

Seed of a subskill still to be written. One element's look; colour roles are `color-and-imagery`, change over time is `motion`.

- **Never lazy.** A drawing made of shapes is modern, refined and minimalist. Simple is fine; careless is not. The stock clip shapes (cloud, smiley, lightning, stick figure) almost never look good: build from primitives, or search a stylish icon set.
- **Every mark means something**, and keeps that meaning on every slide. An unexplained square, circle or odd-coloured dot confuses.
- **A shadow is a decision, never a style.** No default shadow on arrows or shapes; one is placed on purpose, for one reason, on one element (a hard, long shadow to say a strong sun).
- **Arrows** (reference: Excalidraw, formal and sketched): a defined start and end on a node's edge, never in the void; a thin arrow meets a thick one cleanly; no square PowerPoint arrowheads.
- **A graph has well-defined nodes**: no outline, with a label that does not split the node's content, or the node as a circle that does not clash with its content. An edge never leaves from a label.
- **Z-order is chosen**: text is never behind a shape. On an exploded view, labels sit outside and connect to their regions.

Refs: `excalidraw` · `tldraw` · `diagram-design`
